# Introduction to Leviticus

## Overview

Leviticus is the third book of the Torah and central to the Pentateuch. Its name comes from the Greek "Levitikon," meaning "relating to the Levites," as much of its content concerns priestly duties. In Hebrew, it's called "Vayikra" (וַיִּקְרָא), meaning "And He called," from the book's opening word.

If Genesis is the book of beginnings and Exodus the book of redemption, then Leviticus is the book of holiness—teaching God's redeemed people how to live in relationship with a holy God.

## Author and Date

### Traditional View
- **Author**: Moses
- **Date of Writing**: Approximately 1446-1445 BC (during the one-month period at Mount Sinai)
- **Setting**: Almost entirely at Mount Sinai, between the completion of the Tabernacle (Exodus 40) and Israel's departure (Numbers 10)

The book explicitly states over 30 times "The LORD spoke to Moses" (and sometimes Aaron). The entire book consists of divine revelation given to Moses at Sinai to be taught to Israel.

## Structure

Leviticus follows a carefully organized structure:

### Part 1: The Way to God - Sacrifices (Chapters 1-7)
- The Five Offerings (1-7)

### Part 2: The Priests - Mediators (Chapters 8-10)
- Ordination of Aaron and His Sons (8-9)
- Nadab and Abihu (10)

### Part 3: The Clean and Unclean (Chapters 11-15)
- Food Laws (11)
- Childbirth (12)
- Skin Diseases (13-14)
- Bodily Discharges (15)

### Part 4: The Day of Atonement (Chapter 16)
- Annual Atonement Ritual

### Part 5: Practical Holiness (Chapters 17-27)
- The Holiness Code (17-22)
- Festivals (23)
- Various Laws (24-25)
- Blessings and Curses (26)
- Vows and Tithes (27)

## Major Themes

### 1. The Holiness of God
The key verse is Leviticus 19:2: "You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy." This theme appears throughout:
- "Holy" occurs over 90 times
- "Holiness" and related words appear over 150 times
God's absolute holiness sets Him apart from His creation and requires His people to be set apart as well.

### 2. Atonement and Sacrifice
The Hebrew word "kippur" (atonement/covering) appears 49 times. Sin creates a barrier between holy God and sinful humanity, requiring blood sacrifice for reconciliation.

### 3. The Priesthood
The priests serve as mediators between God and the people, teaching the Law, performing sacrifices, and maintaining the Tabernacle.

### 4. Clean and Unclean
These categories teach:
- Awareness of sin's pervasiveness
- The need for moral and ceremonial purity
- Life must be separated from death
- God's people must be distinct from the world

### 5. Covenant Relationship
The refrain "I am the LORD your God" appears over 40 times, emphasizing the covenant relationship that underlies all the laws.

### 6. Practical Holiness
Holiness isn't merely ceremonial—it affects everyday life, including diet, sexuality, business practices, and treatment of others.

## Purpose

Leviticus was written to:

1. **Teach the Redeemed How to Worship**: Show how sinful people can approach a holy God through proper sacrifice and priestly mediation.

2. **Define Holiness**: Explain what it means practically to be holy as God is holy.

3. **Establish the Priesthood**: Detail the priests' role in maintaining covenant relationship between God and Israel.

4. **Distinguish Israel**: Make Israel distinct from surrounding nations through dietary laws, moral standards, and worship practices.

5. **Point to Future Redemption**: Through its sacrificial system, preview the ultimate sacrifice of Christ.

## Key Theological Concepts

### The Five Offerings
1. **Burnt Offering** (Olah) - Complete dedication to God
2. **Grain Offering** (Minchah) - Thanksgiving and commitment
3. **Peace/Fellowship Offering** (Shelamim) - Communion with God
4. **Sin Offering** (Chatat) - Atonement for unintentional sin
5. **Guilt/Trespass Offering** (Asham) - Restitution for specific sins

### The Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur)
The annual cleansing of sanctuary and people through:
- Confession of sins
- Blood sacrifice
- The scapegoat bearing sins away
This becomes the clearest Old Testament picture of substitutionary atonement.

### Clean and Unclean
These categories taught:
- Constant awareness of God's holiness
- Life is sacred (blood represents life)
- Death and disease are results of sin's curse
- God's people must pursue purity

## Relevance for Today

Though Christians don't follow Levitical ceremonial law (it was fulfilled in Christ), Leviticus remains relevant:

- **God's Holiness**: God's character hasn't changed; He is still perfectly holy
- **Sin's Seriousness**: The elaborate sacrificial system shows how seriously God views sin
- **Need for Mediation**: We need a mediator (Christ) and sacrifice for our sins
- **Practical Holiness**: "Be holy, for I am holy" still applies (1 Peter 1:15-16)
- **Worship Standards**: We still approach God on His terms, not ours
- **Community Holiness**: The church is called to be distinct from the world

## Key Verses

- **Leviticus 1:4** - "He shall lay his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him."
- **Leviticus 10:10-11** - "You are to distinguish between the holy and the common... you are to teach the people of Israel all the statutes..."
- **Leviticus 16:30** - "On this day shall atonement be made for you to cleanse you. You shall be clean before the LORD from all your sins."
- **Leviticus 17:11** - "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls."
- **Leviticus 19:2** - "You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy."
- **Leviticus 19:18** - "You shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD."
- **Leviticus 26:12** - "I will walk among you and will be your God, and you shall be my people."

## Connection to the New Testament

Leviticus is extensively referenced in the New Testament:

- **Jesus' Sacrifice**: Hebrews 9-10 shows how Christ fulfills all the sacrificial system
- **Jesus as High Priest**: Hebrews 4-7 presents Jesus as our great High Priest
- **Holiness of Believers**: 1 Peter 1:15-16 quotes Leviticus 19:2
- **Love Your Neighbor**: Jesus quotes Leviticus 19:18 as the second greatest commandment (Matthew 22:39)
- **Moral Law**: Leviticus' moral standards (sexual purity, honesty, care for others) are reinforced throughout the New Testament
- **Clean and Unclean**: Mark 7 and Acts 10 show these ceremonial laws fulfilled in Christ

## How to Study Leviticus

As we work through Leviticus, we will:

1. Understand each law in its historical and cultural context
2. Discern the theological principles behind ceremonial practices
3. See how each section reveals God's character
4. Trace how Christ fulfills these types and shadows
5. Apply the moral and spiritual principles to Christian living
6. Appreciate the beauty of systematic, orderly approach to God

## The Gospel in Leviticus

Every page of Leviticus points to Christ:

1. **Sacrifices**: All five offerings find fulfillment in Christ's one, perfect sacrifice (Hebrews 10:1-14)
2. **High Priest**: Aaron typifies Christ, our eternal High Priest (Hebrews 4:14-16)
3. **Day of Atonement**: Christ is both the sacrifice and the High Priest who enters the heavenly Holy of Holies (Hebrews 9:11-14)
4. **Blood**: The constant emphasis on blood atonement finds ultimate meaning in Christ's blood (Ephesians 1:7)
5. **Holiness**: Christ's righteousness is imputed to us, making us holy in God's sight (2 Corinthians 5:21)
6. **Scapegoat**: Christ bears our sins away forever (John 1:29; Hebrews 9:26)

## Structure Note: The Tabernacle Connection

Leviticus follows the structure of approaching God through the Tabernacle:

1. **Chapters 1-7**: The Outer Court (Altar) - Sacrifice
2. **Chapters 8-10**: The Holy Place - Priesthood
3. **Chapters 11-16**: Preparation and Purification
4. **Chapter 16**: The Most Holy Place - Day of Atonement
5. **Chapters 17-27**: Living as a Holy People
