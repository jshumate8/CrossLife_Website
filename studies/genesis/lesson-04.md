# The Seventh Day: Rest and Sabbath

## Scripture: Genesis 2:1-3

> *"Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them. And on the seventh day God finished his work that he had done, and he rested on the seventh day from all his work that he had done. So God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it God rested from all his work that he had done in creation."* (Genesis 2:1-3, ESV)

## Introduction

After six days of creative work, God does something surprising—He rests. Not because He was tired (Isaiah 40:28 tells us God doesn't grow weary), but to establish a pattern for His creation. This brief passage introduces one of the most important concepts in Scripture: the Sabbath. It speaks to the rhythm of work and rest, the sanctification of time, and God's design for human flourishing.

## Verse-by-Verse Analysis

### Verse 1: "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them."

**"Thus the heavens and the earth were finished"**

The Hebrew word for "finished" is **כָּלָה (kalah)**, meaning "completed, accomplished, ended." This is not abandonment but completion—everything necessary for God's purposes has been accomplished.

- **"The heavens and the earth"** is the merism (a figure of speech using opposites to indicate totality) introduced in 1:1, encompassing all of creation
- **Nothing was lacking** - The creation was complete and perfect
- **God's work was thorough** - Every detail attended to, every purpose fulfilled

**"and all the host of them"**

The word **צָבָא (tsaba)** means "host, army, service." This refers to:
- All the **celestial bodies** (sun, moon, stars - Deuteronomy 4:19; 17:3)
- All the **creatures** populating heaven and earth
- The **orderly arrangement** of creation (like an army in formation)
- Possibly the **angelic hosts** (1 Kings 22:19; Nehemiah 9:6)

The use of "host" suggests:
- **Organization and order** in God's creation
- **Purpose and function** for everything created
- **Beauty in structure** - creation as a well-ordered realm

### Verse 2: "And on the seventh day God finished his work that he had done, and he rested on the seventh day from all his work that he had done."

**"And on the seventh day God finished his work"**

This might seem contradictory—didn't verse 1 say creation was finished? The text emphasizes:
- The **seventh day completes the week** of creation
- God's work wasn't just creating, but also **establishing the pattern** of work and rest
- The **Sabbath itself is part of God's creative work**

Some ancient translations (Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch) read "sixth day" to avoid confusion, but the Masoretic Hebrew text has "seventh day," indicating that the establishment of Sabbath rest completes the creative work.

**"and he rested on the seventh day"**

The Hebrew word for "rested" is **שָׁבַת (shabat)**, the root of our word "Sabbath." It means:
- **To cease, to stop** (from the work of creating)
- **To rest, to settle down**
- **To desist from labor**

Key points about God's rest:
- **Not from exhaustion** - "The Creator of the ends of the earth does not faint or grow weary" (Isaiah 40:28)
- **Not from completion only** - but from ceasing creative activity
- **A model for humanity** - God establishes the pattern we're to follow
- **Celebration and satisfaction** - delighting in what has been made

**"from all his work that he had done"**

The phrase "all his work" emphasizes:
- **Nothing was left undone** - complete satisfaction with the creation
- **The totality** of God's creative activity
- **The transition** from creating to sustaining and governing

### Verse 3: "So God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it God rested from all his work that he had done in creation."

**"So God blessed the seventh day"**

This is the first time a **period of time** (rather than creatures or humans) is blessed. The Hebrew **בָּרַךְ (barak)** means to bless, to endue with power for success, prosperity, and longevity.

What does it mean to bless a day?
- The day is **set apart as special**
- The day carries **divine favor and benefit**
- Those who honor this day receive **blessing**
- It becomes a **means of grace** to God's people

This blessing looks forward to:
- The **Fourth Commandment** (Exodus 20:8-11)
- The **rest of God's people** (Hebrews 4:9-10)
- The **eternal Sabbath** in the new creation

**"and made it holy"**

The word **קָדַשׁ (qadash)** means "to set apart, consecrate, sanctify." This is the first occurrence of this crucial biblical concept.

To make something holy means:
- **Separation** - set apart from common use
- **Consecration** - dedicated to God's purposes
- **Special status** - different from other days
- **Sacred time** - belonging to God in a unique way

Implications:
- **Time itself can be sacred** - not just places or objects
- **God establishes the sacred** - holiness is not inherent but assigned by God
- **The Sabbath predates the Law** - it's part of creation order, not just Mosaic Law
- **A gift to humanity** - sacred time for communion with God

**"because on it God rested from all his work that he had done in creation"**

The reason for blessing and sanctifying is God's rest. The pattern is established:
- Six days of work
- One day of rest
- The rest is **sacred and blessed**

The phrase "that he had done in creation" (literally "which God created to make") reminds us:
- God is the **creator** (bara - bringing into existence)
- God is the **maker** (asah - forming and fashioning)
- The work was **God's initiative and accomplishment**

## Theological Implications

### 1. The Pattern of Work and Rest

God establishes a rhythm for human life:
- Work is **good and God-ordained** (six days of creation were "good")
- Rest is **equally important and God-ordained** (blessed and made holy)
- Both are part of God's **perfect design**
- The pattern is **one in seven** - a weekly cycle

This teaches us:
- **Work is not ultimate** - we are not defined by our productivity
- **Rest is not laziness** - it's obedience to God's pattern
- **Balance is divine design** - not a modern concept but creation principle
- **Regular rhythms** are important for human flourishing

### 2. The Sabbath Principle

Though the Sabbath command comes later at Sinai (Exodus 20:8-11), its foundation is here:
- **Creation ordinance** - predates Israel, applicable to all humanity
- **Theological foundation** - rooted in God's nature and creative work
- **For human benefit** - "The Sabbath was made for man" (Mark 2:27)
- **Points to ultimate rest** - foreshadows heavenly rest (Hebrews 4:9-11)

The Sabbath is:
- A **gift** not a burden
- A **delight** not a duty only (Isaiah 58:13-14)
- A **sign** of covenant relationship (Exodus 31:13)
- A **foretaste** of eternal rest

### 3. Sacred Time

God sanctifies time before He sanctifies space (Tabernacle/Temple):
- **Time belongs to God** - He sets apart certain times as holy
- **Rhythm of sacred and common** - not all time is the same
- **Priority of worship** - sacred time creates space for God
- **Counter-cultural** - challenges our worship of productivity

This challenges us to:
- **Honor God with our time** - not just our space or resources
- **Create rhythms** of worship and rest
- **Resist the tyranny** of constant productivity
- **Trust God** to provide when we rest

### 4. God's Nature Revealed

God's rest teaches us about His character:
- **Self-sufficiency** - He doesn't need the creation
- **Purpose-driven** - He works toward completion
- **Satisfaction** - He delights in His work
- **Orderliness** - He establishes patterns and rhythms
- **Care for humanity** - He models what we need

## Typology and Christ

### Jesus and the Sabbath

Jesus is the **Lord of the Sabbath** (Matthew 12:8):
- He **clarified** its true meaning (Mark 2:27-28)
- He **fulfilled** its purpose
- He **healed** on the Sabbath, showing it's for restoration
- He **challenged** legalistic interpretations (Luke 13:10-17)

### The Greater Rest

Jesus invites us to a deeper rest:
- **"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest"** (Matthew 11:28)
- His rest is not just one day but **continual peace**
- Rest from **works-righteousness** - salvation by grace through faith
- Rest in **God's finished work** - just as creation was finished, salvation is finished (John 19:30)

### The Eternal Sabbath

Hebrews 4:9-11 speaks of:
- A **Sabbath rest remaining** for God's people
- Entering God's rest by **faith**
- The ultimate rest in the **new creation**
- Ceasing from our own works as God did from His

The seventh day has no "evening and morning" in the Genesis account, suggesting:
- The **ongoing nature** of God's Sabbath rest
- Our invitation to **enter that rest**
- The **eternal quality** of Sabbath rest
- The **incompleteness** that awaits final fulfillment

## Practical Applications

### 1. Honor the Sabbath Principle

While Christians debate which day to observe (Sunday as resurrection day vs. Saturday), the principle remains:
- **One day in seven** for rest and worship
- **Cessation from regular work**
- **Focus on God** and spiritual renewal
- **Physical and emotional restoration**

Practical steps:
- **Plan your week** to include true rest
- **Protect that day** from encroachment
- **Gather for worship** with God's people
- **Avoid unnecessary work** - trust God's provision

### 2. Rest in God's Finished Work

Just as God rested because creation was finished:
- **Rest in Christ's finished work** on the cross
- **Stop striving** for acceptance through performance
- **Trust in grace** rather than works
- **Find your identity** in being God's child, not your achievements

### 3. Build Rhythms of Rest

Apply the principle beyond weekly Sabbath:
- **Daily rest** - regular sleep, boundaries on work
- **Weekly rest** - Sabbath day
- **Seasonal rest** - vacations, holidays
- **Sabbatical rest** - longer periods of renewal

### 4. Delight in God's Creation

God rested to **enjoy** what He had made:
- Take time to **appreciate beauty**
- **Enjoy relationships** without agenda
- **Celebrate good gifts** without guilt
- **Practice gratitude** for God's provision

### 5. Challenge the Culture of Busyness

Our culture worships productivity and busyness:
- **Rest is countercultural** - it's an act of faith
- **Sabbath keeping testifies** that God provides
- **Model different values** for your family
- **Prioritize being** over doing

## Discussion Questions

1. Why do you think God rested, even though He doesn't get tired? What does this teach us about rest?

2. How does the blessing and sanctification of the seventh day differ from the blessing of creatures on days five and six?

3. What does it mean that the Sabbath was established at creation, before the fall and before the Mosaic Law? How does this affect how we view Sabbath rest?

4. In what ways does modern culture resist the principle of Sabbath rest? How can believers maintain this rhythm?

5. How does understanding Jesus as "Lord of the Sabbath" change how we approach rest?

6. What's the difference between physical rest and the spiritual rest Jesus offers in Matthew 11:28?

7. How can you practically implement the principle of Sabbath rest in your life without becoming legalistic?

8. What does it mean to "enter God's rest" according to Hebrews 4:9-11?
