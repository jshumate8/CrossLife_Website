# Introduction to Deuteronomy

## Overview

Deuteronomy is the fifth and final book of the Torah, serving as both a conclusion to the Pentateuch and a bridge to Israel's entrance into the Promised Land. Its name comes from the Greek "Deuteronomion," meaning "second law," though the book isn't simply a repetition of earlier laws—it's a passionate exposition and application of God's covenant for a new generation.

The Hebrew name is "Devarim" (דְּבָרִים), meaning "Words" or "These are the words," from the book's opening phrase. Deuteronomy is essentially Moses' farewell address—his final sermons to Israel before his death and their entry into Canaan.

## Author and Date

### Traditional View
- **Author**: Moses (except possibly the account of his death in chapter 34)
- **Date of Writing**: Approximately 1406 BC (the final year of the wilderness wanderings)
- **Time Period Covered**: About one month, the eleventh month of the fortieth year after the Exodus
- **Setting**: The plains of Moab, east of the Jordan River, across from Jericho

Evidence for Mosaic authorship:
- Explicit statements: "Moses spoke" appears over 40 times
- Jesus attributed it to Moses (Matthew 19:7-8; Mark 10:3-5)
- First-person accounts throughout
- Detailed knowledge of Egypt and the wilderness journey
- The book claims to be Moses' words (1:1, 5; 31:9, 24-26)

## Structure

Deuteronomy follows the pattern of ancient Near Eastern treaty documents (suzerain-vassal treaties):

### Preamble (1:1-5)
Identifies the parties involved

### Historical Prologue (1:6-4:43)
- Reviews God's past faithfulness
- Israel's journey from Horeb to Moab

### General Stipulations (4:44-11:32)
- The Ten Commandments repeated (5:6-21)
- The great commandment to love God (6:4-9)
- Call to obedience and faithfulness

### Specific Stipulations (12:1-26:19)
- Detailed laws for life in the land
- Religious, civil, and social regulations

### Blessings and Curses (27:1-28:68)
- Consequences of obedience and disobedience

### Covenant Ratification (29:1-30:20)
- Renewal of the covenant with the new generation

### Leadership Succession (31:1-34:12)
- Moses' final words
- Joshua appointed
- The Song of Moses
- Moses' blessing and death

## Major Themes

### 1. The Love of God
"Love" appears over 20 times—more than in any other Old Testament book. God loves Israel (4:37; 7:7-8), and Israel must love God (6:5; 10:12; 11:1).

### 2. Covenant Relationship
Deuteronomy emphasizes the covenant between God and Israel, defined by loyalty, love, and obedience.

### 3. The Oneness of God
The Shema (6:4): "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one" is the foundational confession of monotheism.

### 4. Obedience and Blessing
Obedience leads to blessing; disobedience leads to curse. This theme dominates chapters 27-30.

### 5. Remember and Do Not Forget
"Remember" appears 14 times; "forget" 9 times. Israel must remember God's acts, words, and character, and not forget in prosperity.

### 6. Choose Life
Israel faces a choice between life and death, blessing and curse (30:15-20). The same choice confronts every generation.

### 7. God's Faithfulness
Despite Israel's failures, God remains faithful to His promises to the patriarchs.

### 8. Holiness and Separation
Israel must be distinct from surrounding nations in worship, practices, and values.

## Purpose

Moses delivered these messages to:

1. **Prepare the New Generation**: Those entering Canaan didn't experience the Exodus firsthand and needed instruction.

2. **Renew the Covenant**: Establish covenant relationship with the new generation at Moab.

3. **Explain the Law**: Not just repeat laws, but explain their meaning, purpose, and application.

4. **Warn Against Idolatry**: The greatest threat in Canaan would be pagan worship practices.

5. **Motivate Obedience**: Through reminders of God's past faithfulness and promises of future blessing.

6. **Establish Foundation for Joshua**: Provide the theological and legal framework for conquering and settling the land.

7. **Call to Decision**: Challenge each person to personally commit to the covenant.

## Key Theological Concepts

### The Shema (6:4-9)
The foundational confession of faith: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might."

### The Greatest Commandment
Jesus quotes Deuteronomy 6:5 as the greatest commandment (Matthew 22:37-38).

### The Prophet Like Moses (18:15-19)
A prophecy of one who would come—ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ (Acts 3:22-23; 7:37).

### The Theology of the Land
The land is God's gift, conditional upon obedience, and must be stewarded faithfully.

### Corporate Responsibility
The entire nation bears responsibility for maintaining covenant faithfulness.

## Relevance for Today

Deuteronomy speaks powerfully to contemporary believers:

- **Wholehearted Devotion**: We must love God with our entire being
- **Teaching Children**: Parents are responsible to teach God's Word to their children (6:6-9)
- **Resisting Idolatry**: Modern idols (money, success, comfort) are just as dangerous as ancient ones
- **Community Responsibility**: We share responsibility for the spiritual health of our church communities
- **Generational Faithfulness**: Each generation must personally embrace faith
- **The Danger of Prosperity**: Success and abundance can lead to forgetting God (8:10-14)
- **Choice**: We daily choose between God's way and the world's way

## Key Verses

- **Deuteronomy 6:4-5** - The Shema: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart..."
- **Deuteronomy 6:6-9** - "These words... shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children..."
- **Deuteronomy 7:9** - "Know therefore that the LORD your God is God, the faithful God who keeps covenant..."
- **Deuteronomy 8:3** - "Man does not live by bread alone, but... by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD"
- **Deuteronomy 10:12-13** - "What does the LORD your God require of you, but to fear the LORD your God, to walk in all his ways, to love him..."
- **Deuteronomy 18:15** - "The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you..."
- **Deuteronomy 30:19-20** - "Choose life, that you and your offspring may live, loving the LORD your God, obeying his voice..."

## Connection to the New Testament

Deuteronomy is the Old Testament book most quoted by Jesus:

- **Temptation of Jesus**: Jesus quotes Deuteronomy three times when tempted (Matthew 4:1-11; Luke 4:1-13)
  - "Man shall not live by bread alone" (8:3)
  - "You shall not put the LORD your God to the test" (6:16)
  - "You shall worship the LORD your God and him only shall you serve" (6:13)

- **Greatest Commandment**: Jesus quotes 6:5 (Matthew 22:37)
- **The Prophet**: Jesus is the prophet like Moses (Acts 3:22-26; 7:37)
- **Hanging on a Tree**: Galatians 3:13 quotes Deuteronomy 21:23 regarding Christ's curse-bearing
- **Elders/Witnesses**: 1 Timothy 5:19 references 19:15 about multiple witnesses
- **Muzzling the Ox**: 1 Corinthians 9:9 applies 25:4
- **Book of Revelation**: Many allusions to Deuteronomy's blessings and curses

## How to Study Deuteronomy

As we work through Deuteronomy, we will:

1. Listen to Moses' sermons as if we're the audience on the plains of Moab
2. Understand the historical context and ancient Near Eastern treaty format
3. Discover the heart behind the laws—God's love and wisdom
4. See how Jesus fulfills and applies these teachings
5. Apply covenant faithfulness to our lives in Christ
6. Learn from both warnings and promises

## The Gospel in Deuteronomy

Deuteronomy points to Christ and the gospel:

1. **The Prophet**: Jesus is the prophet like Moses, bringing God's final word (Hebrews 1:1-2)
2. **Perfect Obedience**: Jesus perfectly fulfilled all God's commands
3. **Covenant Mediator**: Jesus mediates a better covenant (Hebrews 8:6)
4. **Curse-Bearer**: Jesus bore the covenant curse for us (Galatians 3:13)
5. **Choose Life**: In Christ, we choose life and blessing (John 10:10)
6. **Love God Completely**: The Spirit enables us to love God wholeheartedly (Romans 5:5)
7. **New Heart**: The circumcision of the heart (10:16; 30:6) is realized in the new birth (Romans 2:28-29)

## Moses: A Type of Christ

Throughout Deuteronomy, Moses foreshadows Jesus:

| Moses | Jesus |
|-------|-------|
| Mediator of the old covenant | Mediator of the new covenant |
| Gave the law | Fulfilled the law |
| Interceded for the people | Intercedes for us eternally |
| Led through the wilderness | Leads us through this life |
| Brought to the border of the Promised Land | Brings us to heaven |
| Prophet and teacher | Prophet, Priest, and King |

## Structure of a Sermon Series

Deuteronomy can be viewed as three sermons:

1. **First Sermon** (1:1-4:43): Historical review—Remember God's past faithfulness
2. **Second Sermon** (4:44-26:19): Law exposition—Love and obey God completely
3. **Third Sermon** (27:1-30:20): Covenant renewal—Choose life and blessing

Plus Appendices:
- Leadership succession (31-32)
- Final blessing (33)
- Moses' death (34)
